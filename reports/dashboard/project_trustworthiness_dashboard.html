Current Safety Position Evaluation Complete

The selected Qwen 2.5 3B LoRA adapter was chosen using FCT validation only, then evaluated once on a frozen FCT test and a separate held-out Reasoning Fake stress test. The final model is not a diagnostic system and retains human-review routing for risky, ambiguous, or out-of-scope cases.

Key result: The LoRA adapter substantially improved over both the TF-IDF baseline and base Qwen while preserving zero missed hallucinations on the two final controlled evaluations.

Error Analysis and Human-Review Policy

Observed finding Evidence Interpretation Governance action
Zero missed hallucinations 0 on FCT frozen test; 0 on unseen Fake stress test Strong safety signal Continue to measure hallucination recall in future evaluations.
Safe-refusal false positives 9 supported responses were flagged on the Fake stress test. Conservative error All nine were safe-refusal variants for implausible prompts. Route such cases to human review rather than treating them as automatic failures.
Real-world limitation High scores come from controlled Med-HALT benchmarks. Not clinical validation Use only as decision support; require clinical and governance review before deployment.
Routing rule: Any response with potentially unsafe clinical content, low evidence support, fairness-risk indicators, or a safe-refusal ambiguity should be escalated for human review.

Evaluation Controls

  • FCT frozen test data was not used for LoRA training, checkpoint selection, or calibration.
  • The selected adapter was chosen using FCT validation loss after epoch 1.
  • The Fake stress test used question groups excluded from the training set.
  • Results demonstrate controlled benchmark performance, not diagnosis, treatment recommendation, or real-world clinical reliability.
